Gaussian noise

Static has a shape

The “noise” in diffusion is not arbitrary mess. Every pixel is an independent draw from a standard normal distribution, N(0, 1). That one fact makes the whole method tractable: Gaussians add to Gaussians, so we can jump to any noise level in a single step, and we always know what pure noise looks like.

One noise image 784 draws from N(0,1)

Its pixel values histogram vs. the bell curve

Each bar counts pixels in a value range. The line is the N(0,1) density. Resample a few times: the image changes completely, the histogram barely moves. A model can't predict which noise you drew, only that it came from this distribution.

Activity · Spot the digit

Six test digits, each buried under more noise than the last. Everyone writes down a guess for each card, then reveal one at a time. Where does the room stop agreeing?

Keep the number where you gave up in mind. The U-Net in station 3 will take the same test.

4 min

Forward process · noising

Burying a digit, one timestep at a time

Training needs pairs of (noisy image, the noise that was added). The forward process makes them: blend the clean image x₀ with noise ε, with the blend set by the timestep t. No learning happens here. It is a fixed recipe, the scheduler.add_noise line in train.py.

Pick a digit, or draw one

The blend drag t

Same ε for every t, so you watch one image sink.

Noise schedule linear β from 1e-4 to 0.02

√ᾱ_t is how much of the image survives, √(1−ᾱ_t) is how much noise is mixed in. Their squares always sum to 1, so x_t keeps unit variance the whole way.

Pixel histogram of x_t data → Gaussian

MNIST pixels sit almost entirely at −1 (background) and +1 (ink). Slide t up and the two spikes melt into the bell from station 1. At t = 999 the image distribution is N(0, I), which is why sampling can start from plain noise.

Filmstrip same digit, same ε

Activity · Bury your own digit

  1. Draw a digit in the pad. Write your initials if you like; the forward process doesn't care what the image is.
  2. Find the t where your neighbour can no longer read it. Compare with the √ᾱ curve: how much signal is left there?
  3. Why does the schedule keep most of the signal for the first ~200 steps and then drop fast?

Closed form. Each step adds a little noise: q(x_t | x_{t−1}) = N(√(1−β_t)·x_{t−1}, β_t·I). Because Gaussians compose, a thousand small steps collapse into one jump:

x_t = √ᾱ_t · x₀ + √(1−ᾱ_t) · ε, ᾱ_t = ∏ₛ (1−β_s), ε ~ N(0, I)

That's why training can pick a random t per image and noise it in one line instead of looping.

4 min

Reverse process · denoising

The network's only job: point at the noise

The U-Net sees x_t and t, and outputs ε̂, its guess of the noise. Training is mean-squared error between ε̂ and the true ε. Nothing else. Subtract the guess and you get the model's estimate of the clean image, x̂₀.

One forward pass of the U-Net uses the digit from station 2

loss = mean((ε̂ − ε)²) = …

What the model believes x₀ is, at each noise level x̂₀ from a single pass

At high t the estimate is a blur: the average of every digit consistent with that input. MSE training rewards the mean of all plausible answers, not a confident pick. As t drops, fewer digits fit and the blur sharpens into one. Sampling (station 4) exploits exactly this: take a small step toward the blur, add a little fresh noise, ask again.

Activity · Beat the U-Net

  1. Set t ≈ 700, hide the right side of the screen, and let the room guess the digit from x_t.
  2. Reveal x̂₀. Who called it first, the room or the network?
  3. Find the smallest t where x̂₀ is already the right digit. Compare with where the room gave up in station 1.

Training objective (Ho et al., 2020), exactly F.mse_loss(predicted_noise, noise) in train.py:

L = Ex₀, ε, t ‖ ε − ε_θ(√ᾱ_t·x₀ + √(1−ᾱ_t)·ε, t) ‖² x̂₀ = (x_t − √(1−ᾱ_t)·ε̂) / √ᾱ_t

Predicting ε is equivalent to predicting the score ∇ log p(x_t) up to a scale: ε̂ ≈ −√(1−ᾱ_t) · ∇ log p(x_t). The network is learning which direction makes an image more like the data.

Sampling · noise → image

Fifty small corrections

Start from pure noise. At each step: predict ε̂, move partway toward x̂₀, add a little fresh noise, lower t. No digit is hidden in the starting noise. It gets built along the way, and the model decides which digit early on.

Switch to “x̂₀” and generate again: the digit's identity is fixed in the first ~20% of steps, and the rest is cleanup. Most of the image gets decided at high noise.

Activity · Same seed, same digit?

  1. Everyone sets the seed to the last two digits of their roll number and hits Generate. Same seed on two laptops: same 16 digits?
  2. Now press “Same start, new step noise”. The starting static is identical; do you get the same digits? What does that say about how much the injected noise matters?
  3. Drop steps to 10. What breaks first, the digit identity or the stroke quality?

One DDPM step (what scheduler.step does in visualize.py):

x_{t−1} = c₁·x̂₀ + c₂·x_t + σ_t·z, c₁ = √ᾱ_{t−1}·β̃/(1−ᾱ_t), c₂ = √(α_t)·(1−ᾱ_{t−1})/(1−ᾱ_t), z ~ N(0, I)

With 50 steps, each jump skips 20 training timesteps (980, 960, … 0). Deterministic samplers like DDIM set σ = 0, which is why “same start, new step noise” would give identical outputs there.

Activity · Find the clusters

  1. Start on “untrained U-Net” with colors off. Any structure? Switch to “trained”. How many islands can the room count?

4 min

Conditioning · classifier-free guidance

A ten-word vocabulary for prompts

Text-to-image in miniature. A second U-Net, identical except it also receives a label embedding (“the prompt”), added to its timestep embedding. During training the label was hidden 10% of the time, so the same network also learned to generate with an empty prompt ∅. Guidance pushes the prediction away from “any digit” and toward “this digit”.

Prompt → samples ready

8 samples, 50 steps, seed fixed so only the prompt and w change.

The guidance formula

w = 0 ignores the prompt. w = 1 is the plain conditional model. w > 1 extrapolates past it: sharper, more on-prompt, less varied, and eventually over-cooked. Stable Diffusion defaults to w = 7.5.

Activity · Guidance tug-of-war

  1. Prompt “3”. Before each run, the room predicts: what will w = 0 give? w = 1? w = 10?
  2. At w = 1 count how many of the 8 are actually 3s. Raise w until all 8 are. That's the trade: obedience vs. variety.
  3. Prompt ∅ (empty) with any w. Why does guidance do nothing here?

Text-to-image · Stable Diffusion

Same loop, three swaps

Stable Diffusion is stations 2 to 6 with three changes. Pixels become a compressed latent. The ten-digit label becomes a CLIP embedding of your sentence. Label addition becomes cross-attention, so each region of the image can attend to different words.

Cross-attention. Queries come from image features, keys and values from the text tokens:

Attn = softmax(Q_img K_textᵀ / √d) · V_text

Each spatial position picks the words it needs: the “corgi” region attends to “corgi”, the background to “watercolor”.

Prompt builder for Prompt Pictionary

Between attempt 1 and 2: compare against the target and fix the single biggest mismatch first (usually composition or style), the way one denoising step fixes the coarsest error first. Name concrete visual facts, not vibes. ChatGPT's image model isn't Stable Diffusion and accepts long prompts, but the same habits pay off: subject first, then style, framing, light.
